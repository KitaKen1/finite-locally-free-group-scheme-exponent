# Optimal exponents of finite locally free group schemes

Grothendieck asked whether raising every point of a finite locally free
group scheme of rank $n$ to the power $n$ gives the identity. Akhil Mathew's
rank-four counterexample shows that the answer can be negative. Formal
Conjectures asks for the least exponent that works uniformly in a given
rank.
[SGA 3, Exposé VIII, Remarque 7.3.1](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~patrick.polo/SGA3/Exp8-8nov09.pdf#page=26) · [Mathlib counterexample](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679/Counterexamples/GrothendieckPower.lean)

> **Problem.** For each positive integer $n$, determine the least positive
> integer $m$ such that $g\mapsto g^m$ is the constant map to the identity
> section on every finite locally free group scheme of rank $n$ over every
> commutative base ring. Return $0$ if no such $m$ exists.

Write $E(n)$ for the answer. This repository gives an executable function of
$n$ and proves that it computes $E(n)$ for every positive input. A uniform
exponent exists in every rank, and the search terminates using polynomial
ideal-membership certificates and finite-ring counterexamples.
[Formal Conjectures, `optimal_exponent`](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/2de491f90132d3f3fac934edf5bd72acb3dfd15c/FormalConjectures/Paper/FiniteLocallyFreeGroupSchemeExponent.lean)

## Mathematical explanation (AI-generated)

Write $A$ for the coordinate Hopf algebra over a commutative ring $R$.
Multiplication in $A$ is commutative. The coproduct can be noncocommutative,
so the result applies to noncommutative group schemes. The power map has
coordinate expression given by the convolution power of the identity on
$A$. The convolution unit is the counit $A\to R$ followed by the unit
$R\to A$. By extending scalars to the ring of a point and then localizing at
primes, the universal identity reduces to identities for points over $R$,
with $A$ free of rank $n$.
[Lean definitions](Model.lean) · [Localization argument](lean/OptimalExponent.lean)

### A uniform exponent in every rank

For a point $g$, let $T_g$ be its right translation acting on $A$. Applying
the counit to the Cayley–Hamilton relation for $T_g$ gives a monic relation
among the evaluations at $1,g,\ldots,g^n$. Let $I_i$ be the ideal generated
by the differences $g^n(a)-g^i(a)$ as $a$ ranges over $A$. Evaluate the
relation on a product of $n$ factors. Choose the factor indexed by $i$
to vanish at $g^i$. Every term except the leading one vanishes. Varying
the factors shows that $\prod_{i=0}^{n-1} I_i=0$.

Modulo $I_i$, the point $g$ has order dividing $n-i$. In particular
$g^{n!}$ is the identity in each quotient. The ideal $J$ generated by
the difference between $g^{n!}$ and the identity is therefore contained
in every $I_i$, giving $J^n=0$.

The trace of $T_h$ annihilates the difference between a point $h$ and
the identity. For $h=g^{n!}$, this trace is congruent to $n$ modulo $J$,
so $nJ\subseteq J^2$. If a power of $h$ differs from the identity by
elements of $J^k$, raising it to the power $n$ gives differences in
$J^{k+1}$.
Modulo $J^{k+1}$, the square of the difference and $n$ times the difference
both vanish. After $n-1$ steps this gives the bound

$$
g^{\,n!\,n^{n-1}}=1.
$$

The group scheme $\mu_n$ supplies a point of order $n$ over its coordinate
ring. Since universal exponents are multiples of the least one, the
formalized bounds are

$$
n\mid E(n),\qquad E(n)\mid n!\,n^{n-1}.
$$

[Trace identity, uniform bound and divisibility](lean/OptimalExponent.lean)

### Determining an exponent by two finite certificates

Over a local ring, the counit splits off the span of $1$, so a basis can be
chosen with $1$ as its first vector. In that basis the multiplication,
coproduct and antipode have finitely many structure constants. The Hopf
algebra axioms generate an ideal $I$ in a polynomial ring over $\mathbb{Z}$.
Over the quotient by $I$, the same tables define a universal free Hopf
algebra of rank $n$.

For each $m$, the entries of the convolution power minus the convolution
unit are polynomials in the structure constants. The universal power
identity holds exactly when all of those entries belong to $I$. An
identity in the quotient specializes to each local Hopf algebra.
Conversely the universal Hopf algebra is one of the objects that the
identity must hold on.
[Structure constants](lean/HopfStructureConstants.lean) · [Equivalence with ideal membership](lean/OptimalExponentPresentation.lean)

| Outcome for $m$ | Certificate checked by Lean |
| --- | --- |
| Universal exponent | Express each entry as a polynomial linear combination of the Hopf relations over $\mathbb{Z}$. |
| Counterexample | A finite commutative ring table and an assignment satisfying all the Hopf relations, with at least one entry nonzero. |

For the second outcome, every nonzero element of a finitely generated
algebra over the integers survives in a finite quotient. Choose a
maximal ideal containing its annihilator. Its residue field is finite,
and the element survives modulo some power of that ideal by the Krull
intersection argument. The quotient by that power is finite.

Both kinds of certificates can be enumerated and checked. One must exist
for each candidate. Searching up to $n!\,n^{n-1}$ and taking the least
accepted candidate gives the exact answer. This exhaustive algorithm
has a termination proof and can take substantial time even in small ranks.
[Finite-quotient separation](lean/IntegerIdealSeparation.lean) · [Executable certificate search](lean/OptimalExponentExecutable.lean)

### Why rank four is killed by eight

In the commutative convolution algebra generated by a point, write $x$
for the unit representing that point. Let $c$ be the quadratic coefficient
of the characteristic polynomial of its translation. The characteristic
polynomial at $x$ and at $1$, together with the trace identities for $x$
and $x^{-1}$, gives

$$
D=x^4-1=-c(x^2-1).
$$

The second Newton identity gives $2D=0$. Evaluating the same relation at
$x^{-1}$ and clearing the unit gives $D(x^2-1)=0$, hence $D^2=0$.
It follows that $x^8=(1+D)^2=1$.

Mathew's example has coordinate rank four over
$\mathbb{Z}[a,b]/(a^3,b^3,a^2b+2)$ and its universal point has order eight.
Thus any universal exponent in rank four is divisible by eight.
[Upper-bound calculation](lean/SmallRankUpperBounds.lean) · [Mathew's example in Mathlib](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679/Counterexamples/GrothendieckPower.lean)

### Why rank nine is killed by twenty-seven

Over a local base, a rank-nine point is killed by nine when $3$ is a unit.
In residue characteristic three, the uniform bound and reduction to the
residue field show that its order divides a power of three. Write
$h=x^3$ in the convolution algebra generated by the point. Newton
identities eliminate the coefficients of the reversed characteristic
polynomial with index prime to three from the power relations. The
remaining cubic relation holds at every $h^k$.

The trace calculation for the remaining coefficients gives
$3(h^3-1)=0$. Evaluations at $h$, $h^2$ and $h^3$ give a product of three
differences equal to zero. Since the order of $h$ is a power of three,
$h^2$ generates the same cyclic subgroup. The product relation and the factor of three
then give $(h^3-1)^2=0$. Together the identities imply $h^9=1$, which is
$x^{27}=1$.
[Local point orders](lean/HopfPointOrder.lean) · [Newton-identity calculation](lean/NinePowerCalculation.lean)

For sharpness, take the base ring

$$
R=(\mathbb{Z}/9)[a,b,s,t]\big/\big((s,t)^2,(a,b)^6,sa^3-3,tb^3-3,sb^3,ta^3\big).
$$

The two coordinates satisfy $x^3=-sW(x,y)$ and $y^3=-tW(x,y)$, where
$c=ax+by$ and $W=c+c^2+a^2b x^2y+ab^2xy^2$. The nine monomials
$x^iy^j$ with $0\le i,j<3$ form a basis. The group law is
$v*w=v+(1+av_1+bv_2)w$. On the universal point its ninth power has
first coordinate $6ab x^2y+3b^2xy^2$, which is nonzero, and its
twenty-seventh power is the identity. This forces $27\mid E(9)$.
[Base ring](lean/RankNineBase.lean) · [Hopf algebra and order proof](lean/RankNineExample.lean) · [Python cross-check](verification/rank9_certificate.py)

## Appendix: Subcontributions

The algorithm proof uses a universal upper bound and certificates for
each candidate exponent. The rank-four and rank-nine results go further
by providing exact numerical values with separate upper and lower bounds.
The evaluator uses these two theorems to return $8$ and $27$ directly on
those inputs. The exact numerical value in rank eight remains open in
this work.

| Question | Result in this repository |
| --- | --- |
| FC `optimal_exponent` | Executable answer and a Lean correctness theorem for every positive rank. |
| $E(4)$ | $8$, with the upper bound proved here and sharpness supplied by Mathew's example. |
| $E(9)$ | $27$, with a universal upper bound and an explicit example attaining it. |
| $E(8)$ | Exact numerical value open in this work. |

### Reproduce the verification

With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) and bubblewrap installed on
Linux, run from the repository root.
[Lean toolchain](lean-toolchain) · [Dependency lock](lake-manifest.json)

```sh
lake exe cache get Mathlib
LEAN_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 verification/verify.py
```

The runner writes the evidence to `verification/results/`. Pass
`--output` to use a new directory, `--trusted-local` on a trusted macOS
checkout, or `--full-replay` to replay the entire imported Lean
environment as an additional check.

The Lean evaluator computes the three small inputs directly.

```sh
lake env lean --stdin <<'LEAN'
import Solution
open FiniteLocallyFreeGroupSchemeExponent
#eval [optimal_exponent.answer 1, optimal_exponent.answer 4, optimal_exponent.answer 9]
LEAN
```

The result is `[1, 8, 27]`. With SymPy installed, the separate command
`python3 verification/rank9_certificate.py` also checks the two powers
in the rank-nine example.
